## What the uploaded report says

### Human authorship and copyright

The report frames human authorship as the governing requirement for US copyright. It distinguishes prompt-only output from protectable human expression and identifies original lyrics, composed melodies, vocal performance, arrangement, and substantial human transformation as potentially protectable contributions.

It recommends documenting a limitation-of-claim approach in which human-authored material is claimed and AI-generated portions are explicitly excluded. This has not yet been converted into a Steelcore filing policy.

### Generation-platform terms

The report compares Suno and Udio and treats their free and paid tiers differently. Its central operational lesson is that Steelcore must record:

- the platform used;
- the subscription tier active at generation time;
- the date of generation;
- the output ownership or license language in force at that time;
- the human-authored and AI-generated portions of the finished work.

The underlying platform terms are time-sensitive and must be rechecked directly before any commercial release or rights warranty.

### Persona, voice, and likeness

The report distinguishes a fictional persona from a real person's protected identity, while warning that an original fictional character can still create right-of-publicity or false-endorsement risk if its voice or likeness evokes an identifiable person. Steelcore therefore needs an originality and likeness-screening record for voice and visuals.

### Disclosure and distribution

The report describes platform- and distributor-level AI disclosure requirements rather than a single universal US labeling statute. It also highlights EU AI Act transparency obligations for synthetic audio. These claims are treated as release-gate research, not current approved policy.

### Entity, contracts, and tax

The report discusses sole proprietorship, single-member LLC, series LLC, and possible S-corporation election at higher income levels. It also lists agreements and records that may be needed for distribution, generation-platform terms, community operation, public AI disclosure, sync licensing, and trademarks.

These are planning inputs only. Entity and tax choices require jurisdiction-specific professional advice and actual revenue facts.

## Steelcore design implications

1. Provenance must be captured at creation time, not reconstructed at release.
2. Every song record needs separate human-authorship, AI-generated-material, platform-license, and release-warranty fields.
3. Voice and visual originality screening is a mandatory pre-release control.
4. Public disclosure language must match actual creation records.
5. Legal claims and platform policies need dated primary-source verification before approval.
